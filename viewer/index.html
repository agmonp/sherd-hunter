What this is — and isn't

SherdHunter looks for fired-ceramic (pottery) surface scatter at archaeological sites from public satellite hyperspectral data, using sub-pixel target detection.

Why it can work

Firing clay above ~550 °C dehydroxylates kaolinite to metakaolin, degrading the 2200 nm Al-OH absorption characteristically — so fired ceramic is spectrally separable from raw clay and soil. On carbonate (Negev) terrain, anthropogenic ash and lime-plaster can deepen the 2345 nm carbonate feature, an anthrosol halo.

Honest ceiling

A sherd is centimetres; an EMIT pixel is 60 m. Success at orbital scale = statistical separation (an ROC curve, on-tell vs off-tell) and flagging anomalies, not a sherd map. Segmenting pottery types needs the cm-scale airborne/lab tier. The biggest risk is mistaking geology (natural carbonate/clay outcrops) for an anthropogenic signal — every detector must be tested against off-site outcrops first.

Status

One on-tell pixel vs one background pixel processed end-to-end over Tel Arad: carbonate depth 0.0675 vs 0.0457 (~48% deeper). N=1 — an encouraging anecdote, not a result. Next: many-pixel ROC, then a full-scene ACE heatmap.
